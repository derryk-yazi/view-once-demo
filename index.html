Detected events

    Because it runs inside WhatsApp

    Client state cannot be trusted. The in-app browser is ephemeral — storage can be empty on the next open, iOS especially. The budget has to be server-held. That is not an optimisation, it is the only version that works here.

    The page can leave the webview. The ⋯ menu always offers “open in Safari / Chrome”, so nothing can depend on being inside WhatsApp.

    Backgrounding detection is best-effort. visibilitychange and blur fire on tab switches, but in-app webviews are inconsistent about the share sheet and app switcher.

    Bind the token to the msisdn. The link arrives in a known chat, so a forwarded link can be made to fail outright. That is the leak we can genuinely stop.

    What this does and does not do

    Enforced. The budget and the burn. Once spent the asset is not fetchable again — reload, forward or second device all get the spent state.

    Friction. Hold-to-reveal. A screenshot needs Side + Volume on iOS or Power + Volume Down on Android, and lifting the finger hides it. Palm-swipe, three-finger-down and Back Tap still get through.

    Damage limit. The watermark carries the participant and timestamp into every frame, so a leak identifies its source.

    Not possible. Blocking or detecting a screenshot or a screen recording. No web API exposes either, on any platform. A second phone pointed at the screen beats all of it — as it beats WhatsApp’s own view-once.
